This is not a live signal or a paper book yet

Unlike ROBERT, JASON has never generated a real-time signal, never priced a real chain, and has no forward book of any kind. Everything on this page is a backtest artifact: data/jason_trades.csv (824 rows, 2013-2026, reproduced below) and the two research notes it came out of. The sizing section is a calculator against that backtest, not a running ledger - there is no balance to mark to market because no position has ever been opened. The doc's own next step is to start a forward paper-fill log, which has not begun.

What the name means

J.A.S.O.N. - Just Automatically Sniping Options Now (renamed from the research thread's working label, JASON). Thesis: buy DITM long calls on relative-strength leaders that have been pushed unusually far below their own volume-weighted accumulation price (VAP), and exit when price reverts back to it. Where ROBERT trades a 2-day oversold bounce in a leader (median hold ~2.6 bars), JASON trades a slower, ~13-bar dislocation-and-reversion in the same style of name - a different signal on an overlapping universe, sized and run as its own sleeve.

Recommended spec

  • Signal (all on daily close, enter next session's open): close ≤ VAP(50) − 1.5×ATR(14) · close > SMA(200) · RS252 vs SPY > +40% · 50-day avg volume ≥ 500,000 · contract IV ≤ 60%. Min 5 bars between signals on the same name; one open position per name.
  • Gate: no earnings report inside the ~3-week expected holding window - the single highest-value filter found (PF 1.98 → 3.05 on the same trade set). ROBERT's ">7 days from entry" filter does nothing for JASON; the binding question is whether earnings fall inside the hold, not near the entry.
  • Contract: shallowest strike whose extrinsic value is under 20% of premium, on real per-name strike spacing (delta lands 0.78-0.85 as an outcome, never a target). 38 calendar DTE, 30-50 day window acceptable.
  • Exit: close at market when price reverts to VAP(50); otherwise hold to expiration. No DTE cap, no credit roll, no time stop, no early exit on trend/dislocation/RS loss - every one of those was tested and every one reduced expectancy (table below).
  • Execution gate (live, per trade): ask size ≥ contracts intended, spread ≤ 25% of mid, order worked at the mid. Same-day contract volume is not required - deep-ITM strikes are routinely fillable without printing that day.
  • Universe: 496 of the 503 current S&P 500 constituents produced at least one qualifying signal across 2011-09/2026 (broad and dispersed - top 20 names by count are 23% of the sample); see the full list below. This is a different, wider universe than ROBERT's 89-name basket - 9 of ROBERT's 10 watchlist names also fire JASON signals, but JASON was validated on the full market, not a hand-screened one.

Trade count is unreconciled - do not use for frequency planning yet

The rebuild that produced the 1,546-trade headline runs at roughly 2.5× an earlier documented count on the same strategy, most likely from a missing no-overlap convention. Per-trade statistics (mean, win rate, PF) are considered trustworthy; raw frequency is not, and should not be used to size slot cadence or annual trade-count expectations until this is reconciled. The reference log below runs 824 trades over 2013-2026 (≈61/yr on that count) - treat that as one data point, not the resolved answer.

All of the strategy's tail risk sits in the 20% that never revert. Every tested attempt to exit that cohort early (trend break, dislocation widening, RS loss, DTE caps, credit rolls) made the total worse - see below. Two conservative biases run the other way: the engine never charges an event-vol premium, so the earnings-filtered cohort here is flattered relative to reality, and thin early-history earnings data may mislabel a few spanning trades as clean.

Robustness checks passed

  • Volatility crush (exit IV shocked 10/20/30% below entry): PF 3.19 → 3.10, immaterial - 80% of trades exit deep enough ITM that crush barely touches them.
  • Strike-grid correction (real per-name spacing vs. an earlier approximated grid): changed 32% of selected strikes, moved the result +0.13pp - not significant.
  • Earnings filter independently replicated on full historical earnings data: unfiltered PF 1.94 → 2.95-3.19 filtered. Load-bearing, not a curve fit.

Position sizing - $250,000 sleeve, its own rules

JASON does not use ROBERT's 6-slot × 15% sizing. The source research explicitly warns that ROBERT's slot size applied to JASON puts the bootstrap 5th-percentile outcome at −94.5% - effectively ruin - because JASON's 6.5% near-total-loss rate compounds hard through concentrated slots. JASON is sized off its own tail, not its mean.

CAGR and drawdown are modeled, backtested figures on a synthetic options engine - not realized, not fee/slippage-complete beyond the stated f=0.16, and not a forecast. Sizing floors at one contract per slot the same way ROBERT's does, so the most expensive names can price a slot above its nominal dollar target.

Must run as a separate sleeve, not blended with ROBERT. Sizing each strategy as if it independently owned 100% of one account, then running both on the same account, was tested this week in the source research at 110-190% combined deployment with drawdowns of 61% up to total loss. If JASON and ROBERT are ever run on the same capital, their sizing needs to be derived jointly against one shared pool.

The universe - 496 names with at least one qualifying signal, 2011-09/2026

Screened from the current 503-member S&P 500 (yfinance), survivorship-biased to today's index membership. Broad and dispersed - the top 20 names by trade count are only 23% of the sample, unlike a hand-curated basket. Full list: jason_universe.txt.

What is not yet resolved

  • No independent real-marks validation - Databento/OPRA access is gone. Alpha Vantage's close-to-close feed may still work for JASON specifically, since it's timing-tolerant in a way ROBERT is not.
  • Trade frequency is unreconciled at roughly 2.5× an earlier documented count - see above. Per-trade statistics are trustworthy; raw frequency is not, until this is fixed.
  • The 90-name ROBERT-basket-only subset of this universe has never been isolated and reported on its own - if JASON is ever run inside that basket instead of the full market, it needs its own backtest first.
  • No dividend or early-assignment modeling; survivorship bias throughout (current index membership only).
  • The earnings filter needs a forward-looking earnings calendar at trade time - a new data dependency the ROBERT stack does not currently carry for a three-week horizon.

Next step, per the source research: start a forward paper-fill log (20 shadow closes + 20 Paper-Fill Log fills, the same two-legged gate used to validate ROBERT). It requires no vendor and directly measures the one thing backtesting cannot answer - realized fill quality. That has not started as of this page's publication.
