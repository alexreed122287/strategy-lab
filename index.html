What these numbers mean

  • Rank score (0-100) = the average of three percentiles - profit factor, win rate, and avg return per trade - measured against every other arm in your current filter and window. 90 means top decile on the blend. It re-ranks live when you change the Rank window.
  • Arm CAGR is NOT the stock's return. It is what compounding ONLY this ticker's signals under this strategy would have earned - in the trade when signaled, in cash otherwise (low exposure = lots of cash time). The Stock B&H column beside it IS the stock: buy-and-hold CAGR over the same window, so you can see whether the strategy actually beat just owning the name.
  • Rank window re-scores arms on only the trades exited inside that window (Full / last 3y / last 1y). The VETTED badge always reflects the full-window bar (n>=20, PF>=1.3, win>=60, profitable in both eras; an arm with no closed trades in one era cannot be vetted, however strong the other era looks) - short windows re-rank but never re-vet, so a hot year can't promote an unproven arm.

Survivor-biased grade-B tickers are excluded from this dashboard entirely. LEV rows use measured-drag synthetic vehicles. Universe: the local pipeline's grade-A clean-data set (currently names) - coverage grows automatically when new names clear the intake protocol (artifact scan, splice checks, liquidity); the dashboard renders whatever the pipeline emits. Book membership is a separate gated question: expansion attempts went 0-for-65 across x7/x9 and the broad x14 pool failed on liquidity (mid-tier PF 0.43). Vehicle guidance: green = program-validated leveraged mapping; yellow = liquid twin + strong vetted arm (PF>=1.5), a candidate pending the beta/drag validation sweep; "1x" = no twin, illiquid twin, or thin edge (leverage amplifies thin edges into losses - KRE/DPST). Gap Widen arms: per-name records from the validated GW lab backtest rank here too (grade GW; PF/era columns are "-" because the lab reports n/win/avg). Vetted = on the lab's priority-25 list. GW per-name samples are small by design (the book fires rarely per name; the BOOK-level validation carries the evidence), so choosing a GAPW strategy auto-drops Min trades to 5. Min trades defaults to 30 - the program's own reliability floor (its honesty protocol requires n>=30 per window, and x38's kill bar rejected a sample of 22 for exactly this); 20 is the hard vetted minimum.

ⓘ Survivorship audit (PIT panel, 2026-07-30) - how biased are these backtests?
  • Modern window (2019-2026): the numbers on this site stand. Survivorship inflates CAGR by at most ~1 percentage point and per-trade stats by ~zero (43 dead-name trades, t=-0.76). Treat that as a LOWER bound on true bias (reused-ticker deaths and membership look-ahead are unmeasurable in the dataset).
  • The bigger gap is composition, not survivorship: like-for-like, point-in-time full universe 15.7%/yr vs today's-constituents 10.7%/yr - about 5pp from WHICH names are in the list, not from dead ones missing.
  • Deep window (2008-2018) quantification was WITHDRAWN - three stacked dataset confounds. Direction only: a realistic universe keeps the strategy MORE invested and draws down DEEPER.
  • The SMA200 filter is a weakened vaccine: it blocks ~71% of dead-name final-year signal-days, but it leaks (103/937 dead names still fired), and sudden-halt deaths (SIVB-class) are unrepresented - any live open scan still needs a delisting/halt rule before real size.
  • Sizing consequence: armed open-scan variants must be sized to survive a 40%+ strategy drawdown (modern realized -42%).
