Corrected 2026-08-19 — the OOS row above is not out-of-sample

This label was wrong. The 89-name universe's own admission rule requires n≥3 trades and a positive average return in each of two scoring windows, and 08/2020-08/2023 is one of them - the same window labeled "Out-of-sample" above. A name could only be in this universe today because it already performed well in that window. That is a second selection window, not a held-out test.

Measured genuinely out of era - 2010-2020, entirely before either admission window, where a name's presence in the universe could not have depended on how it did there - the universe's edge over a matched control is +0.43pp/trade, t=1.14. That is not statistically significant. Inside the two scored windows the same comparison reads +1.32pp/trade. The gap between those two numbers is roughly what selecting the universe on its own outcome is worth.

The IS-vs-OOS PF gap (4.140 vs 2.396) is not edge decay either: it is dollar-weighted over a sleeve that compounded 9.15x across IS and 4.03x across OOS, while Avg/trade beside it is trade-weighted. Equal-weighted, the two windows are 2.934 and 2.896 - a 1.3% difference where the table above shows 42%. The "in-sample is regime-flattered" reasoning below measured the order position sizes grew, not any change in edge.

None of this means the strategy has no edge - the RS252 gate does appear to do real work inside its scored windows. It means the OOS row cannot be read as confirmation, and the honest edge estimate for sizing purposes is closer to the pre-admission number than to either row in the table above.

What the name means

Relentless Outperformers - the gate: RS252 > +40% vs SPY at the signal. Only names that beat the market for the past year qualify; the ungated version is nearly a coin flip through the cycle (OOS PF 1.157). Briefly Embarrassed - the trigger: RSI(2) < 10 above the 200-day SMA. A two-day oversold print in a leader is a lapse of dignity, not a breakdown. Rapidly Traded - in at the next open, out at the close over the SMA5; median hold 2.6 bars.

On the fills, call him Bob - all he does is bounce.

Chain gate - thresholds amended 08/17/2026

The order-time liquidity gate moved from OI ≥ 250 / spread ≤ 5% to OI ≥ 10 / spread ≤ 25% of mid, with contract volume reported alongside as the fillability read - resting OI is a stock of old positions, prints are flow. The OI floor now exists only for size feasibility (OI 7 cannot fill 11 contracts) and to kill dead books; scale it to roughly 10x the intended contract count.

The reason, stated precisely. The 250/5% floor was never isolated in a backtest: the model-marks run priced options off Black-Scholes, which cannot produce open interest at all, so that gate never bound on anything. It was an inherited assumption that excluded roughly 90% of the basket for no measured reason - and that is the whole case for loosening it. The weaker argument, which this page deliberately does not lean on, is that OI and quoted spread barely correlate with trade quality (+0.11 / +0.17) in the real-OPRA reprice: that study measured entry-mid to exit-mid P&L, which never pays the spread, so it structurally cannot detect what a spread gate exists to control.

Scored across the 89 names on the 08/15 chains, the change is close to a reversal:

Counter-evidence kept in view: the friction work measured 0.80-delta half-spreads running 13.0% down to 3.4% across liquidity quintiles, with only the most liquid tier breaking even - which points the other way from a loose gate. That tension is not resolved here. The 09/01 pilot is the first thing that pays real spreads, and it is what decides whether a loose gate survives contact with actual fills.

Reading the comparison - same signal, different vehicle, different throughput

Win rates are nearly identical in both vehicles (68-73%) - the vehicle doesn't change whether trades win. The calls pay 5-7x per trade (single names move more than indexes on a bounce, and DITM calls carry more effective leverage than a 3x fund) and fire 2.5-3x as often (77 gated names vs four correlated index sources whose signals mostly overlap and collapse to one position). The shares config would keep ~95% of capital in cash at −6 to −10% simulated drawdowns, with far lower execution uncertainty - share orders in liquid index ETFs, though never actually filled either; ROBERT compounds 59-108% at −22 to −30% drawdowns with option friction still to be proven live. Normalised for capital at risk the gap narrows sharply: ~215% per unit deployed for ROBERT against ~180% for the shares config, so most of the headline CAGR advantage is deploying 6x more capital rather than converting it better. What survives normalisation is Sharpe (1.33-1.71 vs 0.73-0.77) and sample (120-176 OOS trades vs 44). The go-live gate

The one unmeasured variable is still realized option friction - the fraction f of the quoted half-spread actually paid. Contract-level P&L has since been repriced on real OPRA NBBO (EXP-001, closed 08/16/2026), lifting the out-of-sample print to PF 2.73 / +8.21% per trade on real marks. But every one of those marks is a quoted mid: no order has ever rested in a book, so none of it is admissible against the fill gate.

Breakeven sits near f ≈ 0.85-0.90 and typical market prints land near 0.16, so the modelled margin is wide. Proving it is the D11/D12 ladder: D11 requires sampled PF ≥ 1.25 at measured NBBO fills with median realized f ≤ 0.65 (bar frozen before the data). D12 then stages capital - 09/01/2026 pilot at 1 contract/slot, 5%/slot after 10 clean entries, full 15% after 25 entries at f ≤ 0.60. The Paper-Fill Log is the instrument. Until D11 passes, ROBERT stays research: no allocation, no x-number.
