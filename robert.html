How the option leg is priced - read this before quoting a dollar figure

These are not fills. No order has ever rested in a book for any row on this page. The dollar column is what the specced contract would have returned at modelled marks, and the one variable it cannot contain is realised friction - which is the whole of D11 and the reason the Paper-Fill Log exists.

Contract. First standard monthly at least 30 days out; shallowest strike whose extrinsic is under 20% of premium. Delta lands 0.78-0.82 as an outcome of that rule. Frozen on the entry bar and never re-selected.

Price - measured first, modelled only as a fallback. From 08/26/2026 the quote is captured at the decision moment: entries by the robert-entry-snap workflow at 09:45 ET, exits by this build, which already runs after the close where the MOC print is. A leg priced that way is tagged CHAIN, carries the real bid, ask, spread, delta, IV and open interest, and pays f=0.16 of its own MEASURED half-spread rather than a typical one. Rows entered before that machinery existed are tagged MODEL and keep the description below. Entry and exit are never mixed across bases within one trade.

The model fallback. Black-Scholes at the underlying’s rv-blend IV proxy - the same number the scan card prints and gates at ≤60% - computed on closes strictly before the entry bar. Validated against live chains on 2026-08-26 for the six names then in the book: mean error −1.4%, mean absolute error 4.5% versus the real mid, no systematic bias. The 1.25 RV→IV multiplier carried by rsi2_call_model is deliberately not applied - it over-prices this basket by 6-12%.

What that validation does not cover. It is a point-in-time check on pricing a fresh contract. Vol is then held FLAT at the entry reading for the life of the trade, so an open position whose IV has moved will drift from its live quote by more than 4.5% - PANW sat 13% under its chain mid on 2026-08-26 after selling off into a vol bid. Flat vol is the deliberate choice: it keeps entry and exit on one basis so a pricing error cannot masquerade as a return, and it is conservative for this strategy, whose exits complete into strength where IV normally falls. The live-chain column is where that drift is reported rather than buried.

Friction. MODEL rows use the locked backtest's published 1.25% in / 2.5% out. CHAIN rows use f=0.16 of the spread actually quoted on that contract at that moment, which is the same assumption applied to a measured number instead of a typical one. Either way f itself is assumed, never observed - see the friction-sensitivity block above for what the assumption is worth.

Sizing. $100,000 sleeve, 6 slots × 15% = $15,000 a slot, floored at one contract so the most expensive - and most leveraged - names cannot quietly drop out of the record. Not compounded: each slot is sized off the fixed sleeve, so the dollar column reads as a per-trade impact rather than a growth curve.

What the ratios need. Profit factor, Sortino, CAGR and max drawdown are all shown greyed until the book reaches 20 closed trades. At n=6 they describe this sample, not the strategy; the CAGR tile in particular annualises 11 days and should be read as arithmetic, not as a forecast.

Corrected 2026-08-19 — the OOS row above is not out-of-sample

This label was wrong. The 89-name universe's own admission rule requires n≥3 trades and a positive average return in each of two scoring windows, and 08/2020-08/2023 is one of them - the same window labeled "Out-of-sample" above. A name could only be in this universe today because it already performed well in that window. That is a second selection window, not a held-out test.

Measured genuinely out of era - 2010-2020, entirely before either admission window, where a name's presence in the universe could not have depended on how it did there - the universe's edge over a matched control is +0.43pp/trade, t=1.14. That is not statistically significant. Inside the two scored windows the same comparison reads +1.32pp/trade. The gap between those two numbers is roughly what selecting the universe on its own outcome is worth.

The IS-vs-OOS PF gap (4.140 vs 2.396) is not edge decay either: it is dollar-weighted over a sleeve that compounded 9.15x across IS and 4.03x across OOS, while Avg/trade beside it is trade-weighted. Equal-weighted, the two windows are 2.934 and 2.896 - a 1.3% difference where the table above shows 42%. The "in-sample is regime-flattered" reasoning below measured the order position sizes grew, not any change in edge.

None of this means the strategy has no edge - the RS252 gate does appear to do real work inside its scored windows. It means the OOS row cannot be read as confirmation, and the honest edge estimate for sizing purposes is closer to the pre-admission number than to either row in the table above.

What the name means

Relentless Outperformers - the gate: RS252 > +40% vs SPY at the signal. Only names that beat the market for the past year qualify; the ungated version is nearly a coin flip through the cycle (OOS PF 1.157). Briefly Embarrassed - the trigger: RSI(2) < 10 above the 200-day SMA. A two-day oversold print in a leader is a lapse of dignity, not a breakdown. Rapidly Traded - in at the next open, out at the close over the SMA5; median hold 2.6 bars.

Chain gate - thresholds amended 08/17/2026

The order-time liquidity gate moved from OI ≥ 250 / spread ≤ 5% to OI ≥ 10 / spread ≤ 25% of mid, with contract volume reported alongside as the fillability read - resting OI is a stock of old positions, prints are flow. The OI floor now exists only for size feasibility (OI 7 cannot fill 11 contracts) and to kill dead books; scale it to roughly 10x the intended contract count.

The reason, stated precisely. The 250/5% floor was never isolated in a backtest: the model-marks run priced options off Black-Scholes, which cannot produce open interest at all, so that gate never bound on anything. It was an inherited assumption that excluded roughly 90% of the basket for no measured reason - and that is the whole case for loosening it. The weaker argument, which this page deliberately does not lean on, is that OI and quoted spread barely correlate with trade quality (+0.11 / +0.17) in the real-OPRA reprice: that study measured entry-mid to exit-mid P&L, which never pays the spread, so it structurally cannot detect what a spread gate exists to control.

The cull is suspended, 08/27/2026. The chain-gate block above still says a FAIL means "cull before first trade". There is no first trade - capital is deferred indefinitely - and the OI floor's own stated purpose is size feasibility, scaled to about 10x the intended contract count. With no intended contract count that purpose cannot bind, so culling ten names on it now would discard forward evidence to satisfy a constraint that is not operating. Note what is actually failing: all ten fail on open interest alone, at spreads of 6-19% (EVR 6.3%, MOD 7.7%, BURL 8.7%) - not on the cost measure. They stay in the basket, keep generating signals, and accumulate measured spreads like every other name. Whether they can be filled is then answered per name by the friction band in the shadow book, which measures the thing the OI floor was standing in for, rather than being settled in advance by a proxy. The threshold itself is deliberately unchanged so the measurement stays comparable across the whole history. Trimming a basket to its liquid names on a proxy is how a hand-picked universe gets built by accident.

Twelve names fail under either gate: DD, EME, EVR, HUBB, IBKR, MKSI, MSCI, ROP, RRX, SGI, SPGI, TPR. Counter-evidence kept in view: the friction work measured 0.80-delta half-spreads running 13.0% down to 3.4% across liquidity quintiles, with only the most liquid tier breaking even - which points the other way from a loose gate. That tension is not resolved here. The 09/01 pilot is the first thing that pays real spreads, and it is what decides whether a loose gate survives contact with actual fills.

Capital is deferred indefinitely - what this test can and cannot settle

Posture, set 08/26/2026. ROBERT runs as a forward paper test on real data for an indefinite period. No allocation, no x-number, no staging ladder in progress. The 09/01/2026 pilot this card used to schedule is withdrawn - not moved to a later date. Nothing on this page should be read as a plan to trade it on a timetable.

What real data still settles. Everything except the fill. Signals are generated on real daily bars, the RS252 / earnings / IV gates are applied as specced, the contract is chosen off the real listed strike set, and open positions are marked against real quoted chains. The shadow forward book above accumulates that record with no discretion in it - it cannot be talked out of a trade, and it cannot quietly drop the ones that went badly. Given enough time it answers whether the signal keeps firing, whether the gates keep doing work out of sample, and whether the names in the basket can be filled at viable cost.

What it cannot settle, and will not while it stays paper. Realized fill friction - the fraction f of the quoted half-spread actually paid. The retired D11 bar asked for sampled PF ≥ 1.25 at measured NBBO fills with median realized f ≤ 0.65. That measurement requires orders that rest in a book. Paper orders do not produce it at any sample size or over any length of time, so D11 is not "not passed yet" - under this posture it is unrunnable, and saying so is the honest version. Every P&L figure on this page therefore stays contingent on an assumed f, and no amount of forward paper evidence will discharge that assumption.

So the contingency is priced instead of hidden. Breakeven sits near f ≈ 0.85-0.90 and typical market prints land near 0.16. Contract-level P&L was repriced on real OPRA NBBO (EXP-001, closed 08/16/2026), lifting the out-of-sample print to PF 2.73 / +8.21% per trade - but every one of those marks is a quoted mid, so it bounds the question rather than answering it. The forward book reports the same way: results across a range of f rather than one number at a flattering one. The width of that band is the size of what staying paper leaves unmeasured.

The Paper-Fill Log stays in place and stays empty, dormant rather than deleted. If real orders are ever placed it is the instrument that would measure f, and it is the only thing that could reopen the question.
