The five filters

  1. Trend. Price above both the 50 and 200 SMA, the 50 above the 200 ("stacked bullish"), and the 200 itself sloping up. The slope condition is what separates this from a simple moving-average cross - a flat or falling 200 with price temporarily above it does not qualify.
  2. Weekly stochastic. %K above 20 and turning up. The source is explicit that the turn is the signal, not the oversold reading on its own; a stochastic pinned under 20 and still falling is not an entry.
  3. Quality. A profitable company carrying sane leverage - the qualitative "could this survive a rough year" read.
  4. Valuation and upside. Price meaningfully below the analyst consensus target, so there is room to run.
  5. Implied volatility. Options cheap relative to their own history. Note this is the opposite of the premium-selling logic used elsewhere on this site: as a buyer of long-dated calls, low IV at entry is the favourable condition.

Contract selection and exits

Once a name clears all five filters, the source specifies roughly 0.70 delta at roughly 365 DTE. Neither leg of that rule is checked by the indicator - both require a live option chain the script cannot reach.

Two mechanical exit triggers: a trend break (close below the 200 MA) or a weekly stochastic rollover. A third exit - "something fundamentally changed" - is judgment and is not expressible as code.

What the script actually measures - real vs. proxy vs. manual

FilterAutomationWhat the implementation really does
1. Trend automated Standard moving-average logic on the chart series. No caveats - this filter is measured exactly as described.
2. Weekly stochastic automated Pulled with request.security(..., "W", ...) under lookahead_off, so the weekly value is non-repainting regardless of the chart's own timeframe.
3. Quality proxy TradingView request.financials() for trailing-twelve-month net income and debt/equity. A loose solvency sanity check - it is not the qualitative "can it survive a rough year" judgement the source describes.
4. Valuation / upside manual Pine has no analyst-price-target feed. The percentage upside is typed in by hand per ticker via an input field, sourced from Market Chameleon or the FMP connector. Nothing validates that the number entered is current or correct.
5. Implied volatility proxy Historical-volatility percentile stands in for true options-chain IV, which Pine cannot retrieve for an arbitrary underlying without a specialised feed. HV and IV correlate but diverge - most sharply around earnings, which is exactly when the filter matters most.
Contract selection off-chart The 0.70-delta / 365-DTE rule needs a live option chain. Not checked anywhere in the script.
"Fundamentals changed" exit off-chart Inherently a judgement call. Not automatable, and not attempted.
ⓘ Why the proxies matter more than they look

A checklist is only as strong as its weakest measurement. Two of the five conditions here are measured by something adjacent to the real quantity rather than the quantity itself, and a third is whatever the operator typed in. That means even a clean backtest of this script would be testing the proxies, not the strategy as the source describes it - and the two would only agree to the extent HV tracks IV and the financials screen tracks business quality.

The IV substitution is the sharpest edge case. HV is backward-looking realised movement; IV is the forward-looking price of options. They diverge most around scheduled events - so a name can screen as "cheap options" on HV percentile precisely when IV is bid up into an earnings print, which is the opposite of the intended condition.

Family - this is not a ROBERT variant

ROBERT is mean-reversion: RSI(2) oversold in a relative-strength leader, held a median 2.6 bars, deep-in-the-money calls at 30-50 DTE. This is trend-following: stacked moving averages and a weekly momentum turn, held roughly a year at 0.70 delta. Opposite premise, opposite holding period, opposite IV posture at entry. Nothing learned about one transfers to the other, and the two should never be evaluated against a shared benchmark.

The Pine implementation

leaps_5filter_screener.pine - Pine Script v6, overlay=true. Plots the 50 and 200 MA, shades bars where the checklist clears, prints a per-filter PASS/FAIL table, and carries three alertcondition hooks (core-four confirmed, trend-break exit, stochastic-rollover exit).

It separates "core four" - the conditions Pine can evaluate on its own - from the full five, which needs the analyst-upside figure typed in. The manual field defaults to 0% against a 10% minimum, so the checklist fails closed until a real number is entered rather than silently passing.

Two measurement choices are exposed as inputs rather than baked in. Weekly comparisons are week-over-week: the current and prior weekly readings are pulled from inside the weekly context in one request, so [1] means "previous week" rather than "previous chart bar" - a naive bar-over-bar test on a daily chart would compare two readings of the same forming week and flicker until Friday. confirmWeekly (on by default) reads only closed weekly bars, so a printed signal never changes afterwards; turning it off trades the forming week - earlier, but unstable. Exit tracking arms from the core four by default, because gating it on all five would tie every exit to the manual valuation field and silence the exit rules entirely on a chart where nothing has been typed in.

Still uncompiled - it has not been run in TradingView, so treat the input defaults as reasoned rather than verified. The TOTAL_DEBT_TO_TOTAL_EQUITY scale in particular is worth checking against a live value: the filter assumes TradingView returns it as a percentage.
